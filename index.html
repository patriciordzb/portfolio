I'm Patricio Beltran, an undergraduate researcher at the University of Texas at El Paso, working at the W.M. Keck Center in collaboration with NASA on GRX-810 — a novel nickel-based oxide dispersion strengthened superalloy manufactured via Laser Powder Bed Fusion.

My research sits at the intersection of advanced materials characterization, additive manufacturing process science, and aerospace-grade quality systems. I've led full characterization campaigns across tensile testing, EBSD, XCT, CMM, SEM, and metallography for components ranging from turbine blisks to automotive heat sinks.

Beyond the lab, I've trained 400+ Department of Defense personnel in metal and polymer 3D printing, developed published standards for America Makes, and serve as Director of Academics for the SHPE/MAES chapter at UTEP, where I launched the S.U.N. summer engineering program.

I'm pursuing a degree in Mechanical/Aerospace Engineering with a Materials Science minor, and I believe the engineers who will shape the next decade of aerospace manufacturing are being built right now — at the intersection of additive manufacturing, data integrity, and systems thinking.

Featured Research · Active · NASA Collaboration

GRX-810 ODS Superalloy via Laser Powder Bed Fusion

Characterizing a novel nickel-based oxide dispersion strengthened superalloy developed by NASA for next-generation aerospace applications. Work encompasses EBSD microstructural analysis at UTEP's MMBME lab, tensile property ANOVA across three EOS L-PBF machines (M280, M290, M400), and a Python pipeline for cross-machine data integrity auditing.
